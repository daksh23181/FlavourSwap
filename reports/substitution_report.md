# FlavorSwap - Deliverable 3: hybrid substitution model

Generated by `src/evaluate_substitutions.py`. Ground truth from `src/mine_substitutions.py`.

## Model
For a source ingredient, every other vocabulary ingredient is scored with five signals, each standardised across candidates (missing data = neutral):

| signal | data | definition |
|---|---|---|
| embedding | Deliverable 2 Word2Vec (recipe co-occurrence) | cosine similarity |
| flavor | Ahn et al. (2011) compound sets | Ochiai cosine, plain or IDF-weighted (tuned) |
| nutrition | USDA SR Legacy, per 100 g | negative RMS distance of standardised log nutrients (energy, protein, fat, carbs, sugars, fiber, sodium) |
| popularity | Food.com | log number of recipes using the candidate |
| group transition | Food.com reviews (non-test sources only) | log P(candidate food group given source food group), add-one smoothed |

Default weights: embedding **1.0**, flavor **0.1** (idf), nutrition **0.5**, popularity **1.0**, group transition **1.0**. The accuracy-optimal weights on validation already include flavor weight 0.1; the flavor-aware rule (largest flavor weight within 1% of the best validation MRR) keeps it unchanged (see *Flavor trade-off*).

**Healthier mode** makes no suggestions for whole fruit, vegetables, legumes and nuts (the foods dietary guidelines already recommend; ranking them against each other by small fiber or sugar differences is not meaningful health advice) or for ingredients with almost nothing to limit (< 0.10 %DV units of energy, saturated fat, free sugars, sodium and trans fat per 100 g, e.g. vinegar, lemon juice, wine, ice: cutting these cannot reach the required improvement, so a 'healthier' swap would only add fiber, such as ice -> guava) and otherwise considers only candidates that are (a) among the **100** most similar candidates by content (hybrid score without the popularity prior), (b) from a food group that receives at least 2% of the source group's reported swaps (fixed a priori; keeps implausible cross-group suggestions such as rice -> garlic out), (c) not a herb/spice unless the source is one (seasonings are used by the gram, so per-100 g comparisons with main ingredients are meaningless; salt -> herbs stays allowed), (d) comparable per 100 g (water content within 50 percentage points, so no dry-vs-cooked comparisons) and (e) healthier by at least max(10% of the source's score, 0.10 units); it adds lambda x relative improvement (lambda = **0.0**). Pool size and lambda are tuned on validation: the smallest pool within 5% of the best validation MRR.

**Health score** per 100 g (lower = healthier), in %Daily-Value units: energy/2000 kcal + saturated fat/20 g + free sugars/50 g + sodium/2300 mg + trans fat/2.2 g (WHO limit) - fiber/28 g. Following the WHO definition of free sugars, sugars inside intact fruit and vegetables and the milk sugar of plain dairy are not penalised (juices, syrups and sweetened products are). As in nutrient-profiling systems such as Nutri-Score, total fat is not penalised (unsaturated oils and nuts are not punished) and fiber is credited (whole grains beat refined grains). An ingredient without reliable nutrient data is never called healthier. Comparisons are per 100 g, i.e. assume an equal-mass swap.

## Ground truth: substitutions reported in Food.com reviews
- Mined from 1,132,198 reviews with fixed substitution phrasings; the replaced ingredient must be an ingredient of the reviewed recipe (see `src/mine_substitutions.py`, tests in `tests/test_mine_substitutions.py`).
- Manual audit of 98 randomly sampled mentions: **97/98 correct** (`reports/substitution_mining_audit.csv`).
- Evaluation pairs: reported by >= 2 different users -> **2,895 pairs** over **755 source ingredients** (5.8% are name variants such as *garlic -> garlic powder*).
- Split by source ingredient: 226 validation sources, 529 test sources (2,108 test pairs). The Word2Vec model never saw review text, and its hyper-parameters were chosen without substitution pairs (Deliverable 2).

## Flavor trade-off
Flavor overlap alone is a weak predictor of which swaps reviewers make (see table above): reviewers often report convenience swaps ("it's what I had"), which do not reward flavor fidelity. Because the project explicitly aims to preserve flavor, the default model takes the largest flavor weight whose validation MRR is within 1% of the best. This rule was added after an earlier evaluation run (before the group-transition prior existed) selected a flavor weight of 0 in a flat region; it uses validation data only.

## Limitations
- Ground truth comes from what reviewers chose to write; substitutes that work but are rarely mentioned count as misses, so absolute Hit@k values are conservative.
- Context-free: the model scores a swap for an ingredient in general, not for a specific recipe (e.g. butter -> oil suits a saute better than a pie crust). Some healthier suggestions are ingredients of the same healthy-cooking context rather than true swaps (e.g. brown rice -> whole wheat flour); the app shows the evidence for every suggestion so users can judge.
- Health comparisons are per 100 g (equal-mass swap) and do not consider micronutrients or fat quality beyond saturated/trans fat (e.g. omega-3 in oily fish); trans fat is partly estimated (see `reports/dataset_report.md`).
